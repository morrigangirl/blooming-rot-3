# Blooming Rot 3: Outline (draft)

*GM only. Full spoilers. First-pass outline for Part 3, written to be marked up. Anything tagged **[DECIDE]** is a proposed default waiting on your call; nothing here is canon until you say so.*

---

## The Shape, Stated Once

Part 3 is a single descent through open Underdark: about **30 miles, roughly northwest**, from the bottom of the *No Sail, No Road* passage to **Erelhei-Cinlu**, ending in **Eclavdra's throne room** with the final battle of the Part.

- The party arrives at **level 9 or higher**.
- They carry a **map**. If they keep to it and don't get turned around, they walk a **true path** of six legs, each closed by a pre-set encounter, and reach the city.
- If they get lost, take a wrong branch, or flee off the path, they drift into **side regions**: an illithid city, a kuo-toa shrine, duergar and svirfneblin holds, drow outposts. Each region has its own ways back.
- **Every mile is dangerous.** Wandering monsters, nests and hazards are checked mile by mile, with tables that change by region.
- **Almost nothing down here is known to the players.** Creatures are described by what the party perceives, not by name.
- **This is the first real contact with drow.** Apart from one unclear glimpse late in Part 2 *(not yet written)*, drow appear here for the first time: traces first, then distance, then up close near the city.
- **Haskur Vandrell** is the only returning face from Part 2, near the end, in the city. Part 3 otherwise stands alone: new cast, no dependency on Part 2's compendiums.

## Where This Sits: The Trial Run for GDQ

**Blooming Rot is Eclavdra's trial run for the plan she later carries out in the GDQ series.** In *Against the Giants*, *Descent into the Depths of the Earth* and *Queen of the Demonweb Pits*, her house arms and directs the giants against the surface, supplies them through the Underdark, and nearly gets away with it. Blooming Rot is where she tests that method first, smaller and quieter, years earlier.

What the trial run tests, and what it becomes in GDQ:

| Blooming Rot (the rehearsal) | GDQ (the real thing) |
|---|---|
| Engineered crop collapse, panic priced in advance | Giant raids that destabilize the surface realms |
| Surface proxies who never meet her (Cindren & Vhal, Haskur) | Giant chiefs who serve a drow they barely understand |
| The administered route below *No Sail, No Road* | The Underdark supply line from the giants' holds to the Vault |
| Illitharia's rot reaching upward | **[DECIDE]** Does Illitharia stand where the Elder Elemental God stands in GDQ, or does the alliance break before then? |

What this means for Part 3:

- **The party is breaking a rehearsal, not ending the war.** Whatever they win in the throne room, the plan survives in a larger form. The ending should make that land: they have taught her what to fix.
- **Eclavdra must survive.** She is needed for GDQ. **[DECIDE]** Proposed: the party wins the throne-room battle and breaks the trial run, and Eclavdra escapes (true to her record in the original modules). A contingency, a fey door Illitharia opens for her, or a simulacrum on the throne are the candidate ways out.
- **Seeds for GDQ** can be planted in the city: correspondence with the giants' holds, a giant envoy in House Eilserv's halls, a ledger of what the rehearsal cost and what the real plan will need. The party need not understand them; the GM should.
- **Timeline:** Blooming Rot is set years before GDQ. **[DECIDE]** How many years, and whether this party could grow into the heroes who face GDQ, or the group that hands the problem on.

## Pacing and Levels

**[DECIDE] Levels.** Proposed: milestone levelling **9 → 12**.

| Milestone | Level |
|---|---|
| Entering the passage | 9 |
| Through the Black Water (end of Leg 3) | 10 |
| Out of the Still Gallery (end of Leg 5) | 11 |
| Inside Erelhei-Cinlu, before the throne room | 12 |

A side region cleared (not just survived) can award the next milestone early. A party that stays at 9 will need the throne-room fight tuned down hard; see *The Throne Room*.

**Travel speed.** In the dark, on bad ground, a careful party covers roughly **1 mile an hour**, so the true path alone is about **4 to 5 days** with rests. Getting lost adds days, and days cost light, food and water.

## Travel Procedure

### The mile roll

Each mile travelled, roll **1d6** on the current region's table. **[DECIDE]** odds; proposed:

- **1–2:** encounter (see the region table; not every encounter is a fight)
- **3:** sign or hazard (tracks, a shed husk, bad air, a collapse, a sound with no source)
- **4–6:** nothing but the dark

That is roughly ten encounters on the 30-mile true path before any set pieces. Signs and hazards matter: they are how the players learn the ecosystem without being told names.

**Nests and lairs** are fixed points on the GM map, not table results. A party that blunders into one finds it every time.

### Navigation and getting lost

Roll navigation **at each fork, after any fight that moved the party, after any flight, and at the end of each leg**.

- **Check:** Wisdom (Survival) by the navigator. **[DECIDE]** DC 13 on the true path, 15 in side regions, 18 after a flight. Advantage while following the map through a leg it draws correctly.
- **Pass:** stay on course.
- **Fail by 1–4:** lose time. Add **1d4 miles** of wandering (and their mile rolls) before rejoining the path.
- **Fail by 5 or more:** **drift.** The party leaves the true path into the side region linked to that leg (see the leg entries). They don't know it yet. Tell them only what the tunnel looks like.

**[DECIDE] How unforgiving.** Proposed default: **forgiving but costly.** Getting lost always leads somewhere worth playing and every side region has at least two ways back to the path, but the days spent burn real supplies. A stricter version would let a party truly strand itself.

### Supplies

Track three things, lightly:

- **Light:** torches, lanterns and oil run out. Darkvision is not light; it shows shape, not colour, and fails on fine work like reading the map.
- **Food and water:** water is the hard one. Mark sources on the GM map. Some are poisoned or claimed.
- **Rest:** a long rest needs a defensible spot. On a bad spot, make one mile roll during the rest.

### Not knowing what anything is

Journals name creatures in a **GM sidebar** only. Read-aloud text and player-facing descriptions say what the party sees, hears and smells: *"a pale shape the size of a cart, plated like a beetle, with hooked forearms"*, never "a hook horror." The players name things themselves, and the names they choose are worth keeping in play.

The svirfneblin, duergar and later the drow have their own words for things. A friendly svirfneblin is the first person who can tell the party what they've been fighting.

## The Map They Carry

The party's map gets them to the city if they read it right. It is **rough, gappy, and wrong in three places**, by design: the mistakes are where the wrong turns live.

**[DECIDE] Where the map comes from.** Proposed: taken from the route below *No Sail, No Road* itself, a courier's route sheet in the same hand as the marks the party has been following. Courier marks along the true path match it; their absence is the first sign of being lost.

The module would ship **two maps**: the party's handout (rough, with the three errors) and an accurate GM map with every branch, nest and water source.

## The True Path: Six Legs

Distances are approximate. Each leg names its **set encounter**, its **branch** (where a drift goes), and what the party should come away with.

### Leg 1: The Courier's Steps (miles 0–5)

The administered road runs out. Cut steps and courier marks continue for a mile or two, then the work stops and the natural Underdark takes over. The silence is the first thing to frighten them.

- **Set encounter: the abandoned waystation.** A courier stop cut into the rock: bunks, a cistern, a ledger with the last entries scraped away. Something has moved in and nested. *(GM sidebar candidates: grick nest, or carrion crawlers.)* First lesson: things here are territorial, not evil.
- **Branch:** the **Fungus Deeps** (wild caverns, lost time, no region of its own; returns them to Leg 2).
- **Takes away:** the courier marks are their compass now.

### Leg 2: The Grey Grove (miles 5–10)

A cavern of giant fungus, spore-light and soft ground. Sound dies here.

- **Set encounter: the myconid circle.** A colony that speaks by spore. Not hostile unless the grove is burned. They can show the true direction in exchange for something (carry a spore-sack out; clear a nest that threatens them). First non-hostile contact below.
- **Branch:** the **Kuo-toa waters** to the west (see *Side Regions*).
- **Takes away:** the first help that isn't human.

### Leg 3: The Black Water (miles 10–15)

An underground river with no far bank in torchlight. The path crosses it.

- **Set encounter: the crossing.** By rope bridge (failing), by boat (whose?), or by swimming (what's in it). **First drow trace:** a hand-crossbow bolt in the bridge post, poisoned, recent. *Nobody has seen who fired it.*
- **Branch:** downstream to the **Kuo-toa shrine**; upstream to **duergar workings**.
- **Takes away:** someone else uses this crossing, and they shoot first.
- **Milestone:** level 10.

### Leg 4: The Duergar Toll (miles 15–20)

A duergar hold sits on the only easy passage north. They don't own the tunnel; they charge for it anyway.

- **Set encounter: the toll.** Pay (in what?), bargain, sneak, or fight. The duergar trade with the city and know its patrols. **Second drow trace:** slaves in the toll pens bear a brand the duergar call by a word the party has never heard.
- **Branch:** the **Svirfneblin enclave** (the best way back to the path in the whole Part; see *Side Regions*), or deeper into **duergar mines**.
- **Takes away:** the city is real, it is close, and it buys people.

### Leg 5: The Still Gallery (miles 20–25)

A long, high gallery where nothing makes a sound. The wandering table here is quiet, and that is wrong.

- **Set encounter: the first drow up close.** A drow patrol with handlers and beasts. **[DECIDE]** proposed: a patrol escorting a slave line toward the city. The party can watch, follow, ambush, or free the line. This is where the players see drow clearly for the first time.
- **Branch:** the **Illithid city**. The silence of the gallery is its edge.
- **Takes away:** a face for the city, and (if they free or follow the slaves) a way in.
- **Milestone:** level 11.

### Leg 6: The Vault Approach (miles 25–30)

The tunnels open into the edge of a cavern too big to see across, lit by a faint glow from below. Fungus farms, slave pens, watch posts.

- **Set encounter: the outer ring.** Getting past or through the city's outskirts to the city itself. **[DECIDE]** proposed approaches: infiltrate with the slave line; enter as guests of a rival house; come through the farms at night.
- **Branch:** **drow outposts** (being caught out here is a side region of its own).
- **Takes away:** the city in sight.

## Side Regions

Each region is a self-contained area: its own map, its own wandering table, two or more set encounters, and **at least two ways back to the true path** (marked on the GM map).

### The Kuo-toa Shrine (west of Legs 2–3)

A flooded shrine, a congregation whose god is whatever they believe hard enough, and priests who are not entirely sane. Dangerous, strange, survivable. A way back: downstream rafts that rejoin the Black Water below the crossing.

*Original material for this module; not a reproduction of any published adventure.*

### The Illithid City (off Leg 5)

The deadliest place in the Part. **[DECIDE]** proposed: **a lethal detour with a real reward.** The city's outer warrens are survivable at level 10–11; its centre is not. The reward is information or a tool that makes the throne room survivable. Thralls of every race the party has met. A way back: a thrall who remembers the way out, if they can be freed.

### The Svirfneblin Enclave (off Leg 4)

The one safe place in the Underdark, and it doesn't trust them. Svirfneblin know the tunnels better than anyone and can put a lost party back on the true path. They are the first to name the creatures the party has met, and the first to say the word **drow** to them.

### The Duergar Mines (off Leg 3 or 4)

Deep workings beyond the toll hold. Grim, industrial, full of things the duergar keep in cages. A way back: the mine's ore road joins the true path north of the toll.

### Drow Outposts (off Leg 6)

The city's outer ring if the party gets caught or wanders. Capture is a real outcome here, not a game over: it can be a way into the city.

### Wild Caverns (anywhere)

Generic lost-time territory between the regions. Nests, hazards, water, and the long dark. Every wild cavern leads to one region or back to the path.

## Erelhei-Cinlu: The Finale

### The city

Erelhei-Cinlu sits in a vast cavern lit by a dim glow. Its noble houses compete constantly, and that rivalry is the party's best tool.

**Eclavdra's power.** In this module Eclavdra is not serving the Elder Elemental God. She is working with **Illitharia**, an **Archfey of the Blooming Rot domain**. **[DECIDE]** Proposed: the rot on the surface was never only a fraud; it was Illitharia's domain reaching upward, with Eclavdra's paperwork as its road.

A fey power at work in a city of Lolth's houses is a heresy the other houses would kill for, if they could prove it. That rivalry is still the party's best tool: Lolth's loyal houses have every reason to help strangers who mean Eclavdra harm, and no reason to admit it.

**[DECIDE]** What the bond is: a warlock-style pact, an alliance between equals, or something Eclavdra believes she controls and doesn't. And whether the other houses already suspect.

**[DECIDE] Entry.** Proposed: whatever approach the party used at Leg 6 sets their first standing in the city (slave, guest, or intruder), and each has its own route to House Eilserv.

### Haskur Vandrell

The one face from Part 2. **[DECIDE]** his role; three options:

1. **Captive.** Still cargo, kept in House Eilserv's holdings. Freeing him costs something and gains a witness who knows the house's layout.
2. **Servant.** Broken and useful, now working for the house. He can betray the party or be turned.
3. **Piece on the board.** Eclavdra brings him into the throne room to use against the party, as a hostage, a weapon, or a lesson.

### The throne room

The final battle of Part 3: **Eclavdra, in her own throne room.** Proposed shape:

- A room built for her to win in: height, darkness, and guards who arrive in waves.
- Lair-style effects drawn from Illitharia's domain: growth and rot in a stone room, bloom that heals her and spoils everything else.
- What the party gathered on the way (the illithid tool, the svirfneblin, a rival house's help, Haskur) changes the odds in concrete, listed ways.

**[DECIDE]** Proposed tuning target: a **deadly** fight for a level 12 party of five that has used none of its advantages, and a hard but winnable one with two or more.

**Victory** means the trial run is broken and Eclavdra is driven out of her own throne room, not killed: she is needed for GDQ (see *Where This Sits*). **[DECIDE]** How she escapes.

- **Eclavdra's name** surfaces inside the city, not before. **[DECIDE]** whether the party learns it before they reach her throne room.
